The Riemann hypothesis and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions

A readable account of the Lean proof in Alpöge’s archive

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge released a Lean 4 proof, found with Claude, of the Riemann hypothesis and of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions: an archive of 175,187 files and 17.4 million lines. This book writes the argument of the archive out as mathematics. It states every result the proof uses, in the order in which the proof uses it, with a proof written for a reader, and gives for each statement the Lean declaration that proves it, with its file and line range, so that the archive remains the reference. The mathematics is Alpöge’s archive; the exposition is ours.

The introduction (page 4) gives the theorem and the argument in six steps. Parts I to V carry the argument from the five statements down to the three inputs that it takes from elsewhere in the archive; Parts VI to IX treat those inputs: Green’s classification of the cuspidal characters of GLd(Fp), the window bound VWin for Tate–Shafarevich-type groups, and the witness HypsAlt, a structure of 46 conditions on one family of data.

How a statement is tied to Lean

Theorem 1.1 (Riemann hypothesis). Every zero s of ζ with s ≠ 1 and s ∉ {−2, −4, −6, …} satisfies Re s = 1/2.

Index of Lean declarationsriemannHypothesis (file Main, line 4)

Statements are numbered by chapter, as in 1.1 or 10.19. The index at the end of the book lists, for each statement by its number, the archive declarations that prove it, with their modules and line numbers; where the archive has several versions of a result, the one cited is on the path of the final proof. The book follows Alpöge’s original archive, the version with SHA-256 9631b4f0…c7fc. Luke Sernau’s grh-lean (25 November 2026) is a shorter Lean rewriting of the proof; the book’s references do not point to it.

How the book was written

The text was written by several AI models, including Anthropic’s Claude, working from the Lean source. The author, who is not a specialist in this area, set the goals and the editorial rules and organised the review: models working in isolation drafted the chapters, and models from multiple families read anonymised copies of the whole book as referees over repeated rounds; every point they raised was settled against the Lean source. No human expert has reviewed the text. Where the text and the archive differ, the archive is right. Sources, the Lean index and the review procedure: https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap. Alpöge works at Anthropic; this work is independent of him and of Anthropic.

Sixty-three proofs, all in Part VIII, are headed “Proof (outline)”; each states its steps and ends with the Lean declaration that contains the complete proof. They are listed in outline-proofs.md. What was tried to shorten the proof or make it more elementary is recorded in simplification-notes.md. Chapter 4 gives an alternative Gaussian-kernel proof of the window criterion that has not been formalized; the archive’s argument is included in the appendix.
