# rh-lean-roadmap

A guide for human readers to the Lean proof of the Riemann hypothesis and of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet *L*-functions, announced by Levent Alpöge on 20 November 2026.

The proof is a Lean 4 archive of 175,187 files and 17.4 million lines, without comments. Several independent checkers have accepted it (see [Sutherland's report](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)), and on 21 November the Lean and Mathlib communities, with the endorsement of the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, certified that the statement and the proof are correct ([joint statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/)). This repository writes the argument out for mathematicians and ties every statement to the archive.

## How the book was written

Each chapter was drafted from the Lean source of the declarations it covers, then checked against that source and revised in three rounds. Eleven rounds of referees (four to fourteen per round, from three model families) then read anonymised copies of the whole book. A twelfth round audited every statement and proof changed in the fifteenth version against the Lean declarations it cites, with three web referees and Claude subagents; each part was audited by a model of a different family from the one that wrote it. A thirteenth round of blind referees read the sixteenth version (seven reports from two model families); in a fourteenth round each point was repaired from the Lean declarations the passage cites and checked against them before it entered the text. In a fifteenth round, long-running GPT and Gemini agents with the full archive re-audited every part and every outline proof; their repairs entered the eighteenth version. In a sixteenth round, a two-layer audit (reading anonymised chapter copies first, then verifying every finding against the Lean archive, with independent adjudication) checked all 41 chapters and all 1,192 statements; its repairs are in the nineteenth version. In a seventeenth round, independent adversarial referees audited anonymised slices across all 41 chapters without access to the Lean archive, and every finding was adjudicated against the Lean source; all 37 confirmed repairs (including writing out the remaining five Part IX proofs and Lemma B8.42bobj in full) entered the twentieth version. In an eighteenth round, five rotated blind adversarial referees audited anonymised slices of the twentieth version across all 41 chapters (with Parts V, VI, and IX drawing zero fatal or major findings), and every finding was adjudicated against the Lean archive; all 42 confirmed repairs, three appendix clarity additions, and an automated LaTeX/PDF reference and citation verification gate entered the twenty-first version. In a nineteenth round, five rotated third-architecture blind adversarial referees audited anonymised slices of the twenty-first version across all 41 chapters under a substantive mathematical rubric, and every finding was adjudicated against the Lean archive; all 24 confirmed repairs (including fixing all 801 moved-proof appendix pointers to print exact clickable page numbers and tightening the automated PDF reference gate) entered the twenty-second version. Each point they raised was settled against the Lean source; the statements and proofs it concerned were added or corrected and checked again. Statements follow the Lean statements; where the archive proves a published theorem in a weaker form, the chapter states the form the archive proves. No human expert has reviewed the text. Where the text and the archive differ, the archive is right; the proofs of a few deep lemmas in Part VIII are still given in outline, and the Lean declarations cited after these statements prove them. Definition B8.10ha gives the Capelli-weighted word forms as the Lean definitions do, with the sign (-1)^(k-l) for each letter, the noncentral component operators, and the distinction between actual tower energies and physical orbital energies. Lemma B8.10hab states the compact form of Theorem P, and Lemma B8.10hbe supplies the compact-slice residual realization, wave isometry, mixed alternating contractions, and terminal finite Gram decomposition; the proofs of B8.10hb and B8.10e discharge the positivity, endpoint, and cutoff-removal hypotheses on the concrete coefficient, wave, and lift arrays. Sixty-three proofs, all in Part VIII, are marked “Proof (outline)” and end by naming the Lean declaration that contains the complete proof; [`docs/outline-proofs.md`](docs/outline-proofs.md) lists them. A script builds the graph of citations between proofs; each cycle it found was read in context, and the text was reordered until no proof uses, directly or through other results, the statement it proves. The typewriter font is JetBrains Mono (SIL Open Font License); put `JetBrainsMono.ttf` in `docs/src/book/fonts/` and compile `main.tex` with XeLaTeX or Tectonic.

## How much of the archive the proof uses

J. Shore traced the kernel proof terms of the five main theorems through a full build of the archive ([data and method](https://github.com/james-shore/rh-proof-dependencies), dataset v0.2.1). The five theorems use 804,912 of the archive's 1,061,400 declarations (75.8%), in 146,287 of its 175,187 files, and the used declarations span 10,737,728 of its 17,374,215 lines (61.8%). Each theorem alone uses at least 804,889 of these declarations. The top of the argument, from the three inputs to the theorems, uses 96,541 declarations in 5,683 files (about 680,000 lines; see `lean/Roadmap.lean`).

## About this repository

- The proof is Alpöge's archive; this repository explains its structure. The explanations come from reading the code and Alpöge's [remark](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf), and the Lean code gives the exact statements.
- The archive carries no licence, so the steps below download it from its author's site and check it.
- An independent author set the direction and made the calls; the text and code were written and cross-checked by several AI models, including Anthropic's Claude. Alpöge works at Anthropic; this repository is independent of him and of Anthropic.

## Checking `Roadmap.lean`

`Roadmap.lean` is compiled inside the archive, so you need the archive, Lean 4.33.1 (installed automatically by [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) from `lean-toolchain`) and the Mathlib cache.

```bash
# 1. Download the 14 parts from the author's site and check them.
for i in $(seq -w 1 14); do curl -fLO "https://alpo.ge/lean.tar.gz.part$i"; done
sha256sum -c rh-lean-roadmap/lean/SHA256SUMS
cat lean.tar.gz.part* | sha256sum
# expected: 9631b4f020199e580ba86178cb56c6e9b09f690c2369a80dc4334aa93f49c7fc
# (the same archive Sutherland checked)

# 2. Unpack (creates ./lean) and add the roadmap file to the archive's Supplements library.
cat lean.tar.gz.part* | tar xz
cp rh-lean-roadmap/lean/Roadmap.lean lean/src/Supplements/Roadmap.lean

# 3. Fetch the prebuilt Mathlib and build what the roadmap imports.
cd lean
lake exe cache get
lake build +Supplements.Roadmap
```

`lake build +Supplements.Roadmap` compiles the import closure of the file, about 26,000 build jobs (the 17,276 archive files it reaches, plus Mathlib, which the cache provides prebuilt). In our test this took about 2 h 20 min with 24 build threads and about 160 GB of disk for build output; check your free space first. The last lines of output print the three inputs and `#print axioms Roadmap.rh_of_three_inputs`, which lists `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

This build checks the step from the three inputs to the Riemann hypothesis. The inputs themselves are proved in the rest of the archive, which Sutherland's full build covers; building the whole archive takes about 1.7 TB of disk.

## A slimmed copy for `Roadmap.lean`

[`lean/slim/`](lean/slim/) holds a slicer and keep lists. Applied to the unpacked archive, they produce a copy that keeps only the declarations the step from the three inputs to the Riemann hypothesis uses, with the structures and elaboration lines those declarations need.

| | files | lines | size |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| archive `src` | 175,188 | 17,374,270 | 1.03 GB |
| slimmed copy | 18,096 | 3,168,570 | 167 MB |

In the slimmed copy, `lake build +Supplements.Roadmap` completes with no errors (25,984 build jobs including Mathlib), and the file prints

```
'Roadmap.rh_of_three_inputs' depends on axioms: [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]
```

The three inputs are hypotheses of `rh_of_three_inputs`. Their proofs are in the full archive and are not part of the slimmed copy. The keep lists contain declaration names and line ranges only, no archive source text. Running the slicer again on the archive reproduces the tested copy file for file.

After step 2 above:

```
rh-lean-roadmap/lean/slim/make_slim.sh lean slim
cd slim && lake exe cache get && lake build +Supplements.Roadmap
```

## Verification status

On 21 November 2026 the Lean and Mathlib communities published a [joint statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/), endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative: "We have full confidence that Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct." It lists four checks: the statement is Mathlib's `RiemannHypothesis`, vetted by experts in Lean and number theory; dozens of people compiled the archive without errors; `comparator` and `con-leche --compare` match the proved theorem with Mathlib's statement exactly; and the exported proof was accepted by Lean's kernel, by `con-leche` and `con-ron` (which carry a formal proof, relative to set theory, that they cannot accept a false proof) and by other independent checkers.

Sutherland's report (page last updated 2026-11-21 12:55 UTC) records the individual runs: all 175,187 modules compile; the five main theorems use only the three standard axioms; `leanchecker` and lean4lean replay every module; nanoda and con-leche accept the full export, and `con-leche --compare` justifies the five statements. Sutherland also joined the proof to [Robin1984](https://github.com/kimihiro64/Robin1984), an independent Lean formalization of Robin's and Lagarias's criteria; the joined proof gives Robin's inequality σ(*n*) < e^γ *n* log log *n* for every *n* > 5040 and Lagarias's inequality σ(*n*) ≤ *H*<sub>*n*</sub> + e^*H*<sub>*n*</sub> log *H*<sub>*n*</sub> for every *n* ≥ 1, statements about divisor sums and harmonic numbers alone, and nanoda and con-leche accept it. At that time Lean's default and paranoid kernels were replaying the export and an end-to-end `lake comparator` run was in progress. The report has the current state.
